## 4. Key Questions & Provenance Assessment

### A. Can the real Maitri wind-speed data replace the synthetic wind profile directly?
**No, not directly as raw numbers.**  
The real Maitri weather dataset records meteorological **wind speed in meters per second ($\text{m/s}$)**, while the optimizer mathematical formulation requires **electrical power in kilowatts ($\text{kW}$)**. Feeding $\text{m/s}$ directly into the power balance would cause a physical unit dimensional error ($15\text{ m/s} \neq 15\text{ kW}$). An aerodynamic-to-electrical turbine conversion pipeline $P(v)$ is necessary.

---

### B. What information is still missing to convert wind speed into electrical power?
To convert empirical wind velocity $v$ ($\text{m/s}$) into electrical generation $P$ ($\text{kW}$), the following engineering specifications are required:
1. **Turbine Nameplate Capacity ($P_{\text{rated}}$ in $\text{kW}$)** and number of installed turbine units.
2. **Turbine Power Curve Characteristic Points**:
   - **Cut-in wind speed ($v_{\text{cut-in}}$)**: Minimum velocity to initiate rotation (typically $3.0\text{–}3.5\text{ m/s}$). Below this, $P(v) = 0$.
   - **Rated wind speed ($v_{\text{rated}}$)**: Minimum velocity at which the turbine reaches nameplate output (typically $11.0\text{–}13.0\text{ m/s}$).
   - **Cut-out / Storm Survival speed ($v_{\text{cut-out}}$)**: High-wind velocity where the aerodynamic brake / furling system shuts down the rotor to prevent mechanical destruction (typically $25.0\text{ m/s}$).
3. **Antarctic Air Density Correction ($\rho$)**:
   - Standard sea-level test density is $\rho_0 = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$.
   - Sub-zero Antarctic temperatures (mean $-10^\circ\text{C}$ to $-25^\circ\text{C}$) increase air density to $\rho \approx 1.30\text{–}1.38\text{ kg/m}^3$, increasing kinetic wind power density ($\frac{1}{2}\rho v^3$) by $8\text{–}12\%$.
4. **Cold-Climate Icing / Derating Factor ($\eta_{\text{ice/loss}}$)**:
   - Blade rime ice accumulation and drivetrain low-temperature lubricant losses (typically $0.85\text{–}0.95$).

---

### C. What turbine assumptions would we need?
Following the same disciplined approach used for the $100\text{ kW}$ PV model, a transparent **Wind Configuration** (e.g., `ml-service/config/maitri_wind_config.json`) should parameterize:
- **Baseline Scenario Capacity**: e.g., $50.0\text{ kW}$ total wind capacity (e.g., $2 \times 25\text{ kW}$ polar-grade turbines).
- **Standard Piecewise Aerodynamic Power Curve**:
  $$P(v) = \begin{cases} 0 & v < v_{\text{cut-in}} \quad (v < 3.5\text{ m/s}) \\ P_{\text{rated}} \cdot \left(\frac{v - v_{\text{cut-in}}}{v_{\text{rated}} - v_{\text{cut-in}}}\right)^3 & 3.5\text{ m/s} \le v < 12.0\text{ m/s} \\ P_{\text{rated}} & 12.0\text{ m/s} \le v < 25.0\text{ m/s} \\ 0 & v \ge 25.0\text{ m/s} \quad (\text{High-Wind Storm Cut-Out}) \end{cases}$$
- **Turbine Availability / System Efficiency**: $\eta = 0.90$.
